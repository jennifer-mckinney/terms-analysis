# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Anyone who signs agreements they don't fully understand: parents managing family subscriptions, students on new platforms, vibe coders auditing vendor APIs, compliance officers at nonprofits and government agencies, privacy advocates, and general consumers. Users arrive with varying technical and legal literacy but a shared need — to understand what they're agreeing to, quickly and without a law degree.

## Product Purpose

A privacy-first local tool that reads Terms of Service and Privacy Policies and translates them into plain language risk assessments. It identifies high-risk clauses, maps compliance requirements to jurisdictions, and gives users the confidence to make informed consent decisions. Success means a nervous non-expert finishing a review and actually understanding what they learned.

## Brand Personality

Clear, Calm, Empowering. The tool handles serious subject matter (legal risk, data privacy) but should never feel alarming, bureaucratic, or exclusive. Think trusted guide, not threat scanner. Think "I've got you," not "WARNING: CRITICAL RISK."

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS blue-gradient dashboard (Salesforce-style, enterprise compliance tools)
- Legal document clutter — walls of tiny text, dense tables without hierarchy
- Security scanner alarm aesthetic — red-alert, threat-feed visual language, aggressive risk banners
- Anything that implies the user needs to be an expert to use it

## Design Principles

1. **Plain language first** — Every piece of risk information should be readable by a thoughtful non-lawyer. If the UI makes risk feel complex, it's failing.
2. **Calm confidence** — The tone and visual weight communicate: this is manageable, you are informed. Not "be afraid," but "here's what you need to know."
3. **Accessible to everyone** — The target user is not a developer or compliance officer by default. Visual hierarchy, contrast, and copy must work for a wide range of literacy levels and devices.
4. **Trust through transparency** — The tool runs locally, respects privacy, and shows its work (confidence scores, evidence, jurisdictions). The UI should reinforce that trust, not obscure it.
5. **Low friction, high signal** — Reduce steps to insight. Prioritize the findings that matter most. Don't make users dig for their answer.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Broad audience with varying literacy; err toward plain language, generous touch targets, and strong contrast. Reduced motion variants required for all animations.
